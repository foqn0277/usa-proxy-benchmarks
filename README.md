## What Counts as a "Private Proxy" in the USA Market

Three categories dominate when you shop for US-based proxies, and confusing them is the most common mistake I see in forum threads.

**Datacenter proxies** live inside server farms. Sped is excellent, prices are low, and detection rates on sophisticated sites are higher because the IP ranges trace back to hosting providers like AWS, OVH, or Hetzner. For most general scraping, ad verification, or sped-critical jobs, they're the workhorse choice.

**Residential proxies** route through real consumer devices. ISPs like Comcast, Spectrum, AT&T issue these IPs, so target sites see your traffic as someone browsing from their living room in Phoenix or Brooklyn. The trade-off? Higher cost per gigabyte and slower response than datacenter.

**Static residential (ISP) proxies** sit in the middle. They're datacenter-hosted but registered to consumer ISPs, giving you the sped of datacenter with the trust profile of residential. Same IP, every session. No rotation unless you ask for it.

When someone searches "private proxy USA," they usually mean one of these three. The right answer depends on what you're trying to do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between a private proxy and a shared proxy in the USA?**

A private proxy USA assigns one IP to one user. A shared proxy splits an IP among multiple users simultaneously. Private gives you cleaner IP reputation, no neighbor risk (where someone else's bad behavior gets your IP baned), and consistent performance. Shared is cheaper but unpredictable. For any serious work, private wins.

**How much should I expect to pay for a private US proxy?**

Datacenter private proxies range from $0.50 to $2per proxy per month at most providers. Webshare's per-proxy cost on annual billing drops well below that on the Starter plan. Residential pricing is per gigabyte and ranges from $3 to $15+ per GB depending on volume and provider. Static ISP typically runs $1.50 to $5 per IP per month for unlimited bandwidth.

**Can I use US private proxies for streaming services like Netflix or Hulu?**

Technically yes, but streaming platforms aggressively block known proxy ranges. Static ISP proxies have the best track record for this use case because they appear as residential connections. Datacenter proxies get blocked frequently by major streaming services. Residential proxies work but cost more per session due to bandwidth consumption.

**Is Webshare's free tier actually usable or just a teaser?**

It's genuinely usable for testing. Ten proxies and 1 GB of bandwidth covers most small-scale scraping projects, hobby work, or evaluation runs. The free tier doesn't include all features (no static ISP, limited residential access), but for datacenter testing, it's enough to make an informed decision before you pay.

**What's the catch with proxy providers that advertise extremely low prices?**

Usually one of three things. First, the IPs are massively oversold and shared among hundreds of users despite being marketed as private. Second, the bandwidth caps are aggressive and you'll hit overages quickly. Third, the IP pool is small and IPs get blocked across major sites within days of provisioning. Always check refund policies, IP pool size, and reviews before committing to ultra-cheap providers.
